Our story

Medical Literacy through Language (MLL) is a youth-led, volunteer-run initiative founded by San Diego students passionate about Classical languages and their practical applications in modern life. What began as a shared enthusiasm for Latin and Greek has grown into an outreach effort aimed at one of the most persistent barriers in healthcare: the language itself.

We are high schoolers who grew up loving the classics — tracing etymologies, puzzling over declensions, and reading texts written thousands of years ago. For a long time that passion felt purely academic, something that lived inside the classroom. The turning point came when we realized our interests could have a practical application in helping our communities.

Armed with the knowledge that much of Western medical language is rooted in ancient Greek and Latin morphemes, we founded this initiative to help communities better understand medical language and navigate healthcare with greater confidence. The same roots we studied for pleasure turn out to be the key to words like tachycardia, nephritis, and hypoglycemia — and we believe that key belongs to everyone, not only to the people in white coats.

Our mission

“Our mission is to empower communities to navigate the healthcare system through understanding medical language by revealing the timeless classical roots hidden inside every term.”

Our values

  • Clarity — clear communication shouldn't be a privilege. If an explanation only works for people who already understand it, it isn't an explanation.
  • Access — language barriers compound with every other barrier to care. We work to make understanding free, portable, and available to anyone who wants it.
  • Curiosity — we came to this through a love of the classics, and we think decoding a word should feel less like studying and more like solving a puzzle.
